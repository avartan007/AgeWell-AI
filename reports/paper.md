# Preliminary Technical Research Manuscript: Missingness-Aware and Uncertainty-Calibrated Machine Learning for Predicting Functional Decline in Longitudinal Aging Cohorts

**Author:** Avartan (B.E. Computer Engineering, Savitribai Phule Pune University)  
**Target:** MEXT University Recommendation Research Portfolio (October 2027 Enrollment)

## Abstract
**Background:** Early identification of older adults at risk of functional decline in Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) can inform preventive community interventions. However, longitudinal aging surveys exhibit substantial informative missingness (MAR/MNAR) in physical and cognitive assessments, and standard machine learning classifiers frequently produce miscalibrated probability estimates under temporal wave shift.  
**Methods:** We developed a leakage-safe longitudinal prediction pipeline (`AgeWell-AI`) enforcing disjoint participant-grouped temporal splits (training on Waves 1–2, calibrating on held-out validation participants, and evaluating out-of-time on Wave 3→4). We evaluated $L_2$-penalized Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosted Decision Trees across four pre-specified hypotheses (H1–H4) on a harmonized HRS/JAGES-structured synthetic cohort (`n = 3,892` eligible wave-pairs across `1,734` baseline ADL-intact older adults aged $\ge 65$).  
**Results:** Incorporating explicit missingness indicators ($M_{ij}$) alongside train-only median imputation improved out-of-time AUPRC from `0.5454` to `0.5675` (Logistic Regression) and from `0.5510` to `0.5682` (Random Forest), while maintaining robustness under `+40%` simulated MNAR missingness (`AUROC 0.7523 vs. 0.7327` for Logistic Regression; `Sensitivity@Top20% 0.4048 vs. 0.3452` for Random Forest). Post-hoc Sigmoid calibration fitted on validation folds reduced Missing-Aware Random Forest Brier score by `20.3%` (`0.2220 → 0.1770`) and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) by `75.4%` (`0.2055 → 0.0505`). Temporal wave splitting revealed a `2.76` percentage-point AUROC optimism gap relative to same-period random participant splits (`0.7440 vs. 0.7716`). Subgroup auditing identified lower discrimination among older adults living alone (`AUROC 0.6568 vs. 0.7849` co-residing).  
**Conclusions:** Missingness-aware features and validation-tuned probability calibration substantially improve reliability and MNAR robustness in longitudinal functional-decline prediction, provided that missingness mechanisms remain stable across waves and high-uncertainty cases are routed to human triage rather than passively excluded.
